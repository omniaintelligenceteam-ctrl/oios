# Content Drafts

Write LinkedIn posts and short-form content in Wes's voice — casual, confident, real. No corporate garbage.

## Trigger
User says things like: "write a LinkedIn post about [topic]", "draft some content about [thing]", "help me post about [experience/story/insight]"

## Process

### Step 1: Get the brief
Ask (if not provided):
- What's the topic, story, or point to make?
- Platform: LinkedIn, Twitter/X, Instagram, or other?
- Goal: build authority, attract clients, share a win, tell a story?
- Any specific angle or hook they have in mind?

### Step 2: Draft the content

**Wes's voice:**
- Casual, direct, no bullshit
- Talks like a real person, not a brand
- Shares real experiences and opinions
- Makes AI feel accessible and exciting, not scary or overly technical
- Confident — he knows his stuff

**LinkedIn format (performs best):**
```
[Hook — first line must stop the scroll. Bold claim, surprising stat, or real story opening.]

[2-3 short paragraphs or punchy lines that develop the idea]

[The real point or takeaway]

[Optional CTA — question to the audience, or soft pitch]

#[1-3 relevant hashtags max]
```

**Rules:**
- First line is everything. Rewrite it until it's good.
- Short paragraphs. Single sentences are fine.
- No "Excited to share..." or "Thrilled to announce..."
- No jargon. If a 12-year-old wouldn't understand it, rewrite it.
- End with something that makes people want to comment or connect

### Step 3: Offer variations
Always provide:
- One version (main draft)
- Offer: "Want a more personal/story-driven version? Or a more direct/punchy one?"

### Step 4: Optional series
If the topic has legs, ask: "This could be a 3-post series. Want me to map that out?"

## Notes
- Save posts that get good engagement to `references/examples/` to inform future drafts.
- Consistency of voice matters more than perfection. If it sounds like Wes, it's good.
- Never ghostwrite in a way that sounds fake. If it doesn't sound like something Wes would actually say, rewrite it.
