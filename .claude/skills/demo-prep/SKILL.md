# Demo Prep

Build and customize a demo script showing a specific business exactly what Claude + OpenClaw can do for them. Goal: make it a no-brainer close.

## Trigger
User says things like: "prep a demo for [company]", "help me build a demo for [industry/prospect]", "I have a demo with [name] on [date]"

## Process

### Step 1: Get the context
Ask Wes (if not provided):
- Company name and what they do
- Who's in the room? (CEO, ops lead, etc.)
- What are their biggest pain points or time-wasters?
- What's their current tech stack? (any tools they use)
- Any previous conversations or objections we know about?
- How long is the demo? (15 min, 30 min, 1 hour)

### Step 2: Build the demo script

Structure:
```
## Demo: [Company Name]
**Date:** [date]
**Audience:** [who's in the room]
**Duration:** [X minutes]

---

### Opening Hook (2 min)
[Personalized opening — reference their specific situation, not a generic intro]
"I want to show you exactly how [Company Name] could [specific outcome] without adding headcount."

### The Problem Frame (3 min)
[Name the specific pain points they have — validate before pitching]
- [Pain point 1]
- [Pain point 2]

### The Demo (bulk of time)
[3-5 specific use cases mapped to their business — show, don't tell]

Use case 1: [Title]
- What to show: [exact demo steps]
- What to say: [talking points]
- Why it matters to them: [tie to their pain]

Use case 2: [Title]
...

### The Close (3-5 min)
[What life looks like after they work with Omnia]
[CTA — what's the next step?]

---

### Objection Prep
- "[Likely objection 1]" → [Response]
- "[Likely objection 2]" → [Response]
```

### Step 3: Deliver + offer follow-up
- Present the full script
- Ask: "Want me to also draft the pre-demo email and post-demo follow-up?"
- Suggest saving a customized version to `projects/ceo-demo/` if it's worth keeping as a template

## Notes
- Every demo should feel like it was built specifically for that company — because it was.
- Use their industry language. Do a quick research pass if needed.
- Less features, more outcomes. CEOs don't care how it works. They care what it does for them.
