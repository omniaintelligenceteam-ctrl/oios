# Follow-Up

Draft follow-up messages after demos, calls, or outreach. Keep the conversation alive without being annoying.

## Trigger
User says things like: "write a follow-up for [prospect]", "draft a follow-up after my demo with [company]", "they haven't responded — help me follow up"

## Process

### Step 1: Get context
Ask Wes (if not provided):
- Who is this for? (name, company)
- What happened in the last interaction? (demo, call, email — brief summary)
- How long since last contact?
- Any objections or hesitations they mentioned?
- What's the desired outcome of this follow-up? (book next call, get a decision, re-engage cold lead)

### Step 2: Pick the right sequence stage

**Stage 1 — Post-demo (same day or next day):**
- Thank them for their time (one line, not gushing)
- Recap the 1-2 things that landed
- Restate the specific value for their situation
- Clear CTA: next step

**Stage 2 — Check-in (3-5 days no response):**
- Short. Like 4-5 sentences max.
- Don't re-pitch. Just nudge.
- Add a new piece of value (a relevant example, a quick insight)
- Easy yes/no question as CTA

**Stage 3 — Breakup (10-14 days no response):**
- Keep the door open, don't burn it
- Light humor works
- Give them an easy out while leaving them curious

### Step 3: Draft and present

**Voice rules:**
- Casual. Not salesy.
- Never say "just checking in" or "circling back"
- Be direct about what you want from them
- Keep it short — under 100 words for stages 2 and 3

### Step 4: Offer the full sequence
Ask: "Want me to draft all 3 stages now so they're ready to go?"

## Notes
- If Wes gave you objections from the demo, address them directly in Stage 1.
- Save effective follow-up templates to `references/examples/`.
- If a lead goes cold past Stage 3, archive them and revisit in 30-60 days.
